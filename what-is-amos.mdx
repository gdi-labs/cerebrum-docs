What you previously assembled out of people, processes, and tools — the structure of the company itself — Amos encodes as policy, executes as an AI workforce, and produces as an auditable record. It assumes humans leave the operational center and need a system to **govern an AI workforce from above**.

## The problem Amos replaces

Small teams lose ideas that never ship. Building a working engineering function the traditional way takes six months or more — assuming the hires exist and stay. Typical retention before accumulated context walks out the door is 12–24 months. And async context silently fragments across seven-plus tools every day.

Amos replaces that fragility with a system: context that accumulates and persists, orchestration that plans, builds, and verifies, and an auditable record of everything it did.

## How Amos compares

**Cursor, Copilot, ChatGPT.** Productivity tools. They augment engineers you already have — they make the engineers you have faster, but they don't give you an engineering function.

**Devin, Perplexity and autonomous agents.** Designed to hand off tasks in engineering organizations that already exist. They assume the team, the process, and the oversight are already in place.

**Amos replaces the engineering function itself** — for small teams that don't yet have one and can't afford to build one from scratch. The division of labor is the point: **Amos runs the function. You run the strategy.** You stay involved at the governance points where your judgment matters: choosing between architectural paths, approving decisions that affect users, reviewing output before it ships.

## The four commitments

The pillars Amos was built on:

1. **Persistent knowledge.** Ingested from Slack, Notion, GitHub, Figma, transcripts, and email. When engineers leave, context stays.
2. **Multi-agent hierarchy.** Planning, execution, and verification separated across four levels. How reliable engineering teams work.
3. **Cost-aware dispatch.** Routine work runs on free local models. Frontier models handle high-value reasoning. Every call tracked.
4. **Audit-ready by default.** Every action emits a typed event. Every call cost-tracked per tenant. Not a bolt-on. The design center.

## Built with Amos: Pike

Pike is a modular DeFi money market and DEX liquidity venue, built end to end with Amos and deployed to production on Base — smart contracts, backend, oracle integration, frontend, and the audit trail. Not a demo.

Amos reviews open-source codebases and monitors the security landscape, incorporating battle-tested patterns into how it builds. For smart contracts holding user funds, independent audits and formal verification remain essential — Amos complements that process rather than replacing it.
