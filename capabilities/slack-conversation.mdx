## What it enables

- **Talk to the bot and get an answer.** DM the Amos app, or `@Amos` in any
  channel it is invited to, and the reply comes back in the thread — routed
  through the same routing the website's "Ask anything" uses, so the answer is
  the answer, not a Slack-specific subset.
- **Threads are conversations.** One Slack thread is one Amos conversation: a
  follow-up reply continues with prior turns in context, and the same
  conversation is visible on the website, owned by the same person.
- **Mid-run questions get answered where they were asked.** When a job pauses
  on a human gate, Amos asks in the thread — buttons where one click can
  express the answer, a numbered list where it cannot — and either a click or
  a typed reply resumes the job.
- **Approval buttons work, both ways.** Approve or reject from Slack; when the
  decision happens — from Slack *or* from the web inbox — the DM updates to say
  what was decided and by whom, in plain language, and its buttons stop
  looking live. You can also answer an approval **by typing in its thread**,
  not only by clicking; if the web inbox and Slack race, the first decision
  wins and the loser is told.

## What Amos listens to

In a channel, Amos can see every message — that is how it follows a thread
it is already talking in. Seeing and acting on are two different questions.

**Amos acts on:** a DM, an `@mention`, and a reply inside a thread it already
holds a conversation in. A top-level channel message is never a follow-up.

**Amos says nothing at all** for everything else, including when something is
wrong. Two colleagues talking to each other in a thread Amos happens to be in
will not draw a reply — it only speaks when it was spoken *to*.

It also never reacts to:

| Skipped | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Its own replies and every app post | the self-loop guard |
| Slackbot posts (huddle notes, reminders) | Slackbot announces as a user, so it needs its own rule |
| File attachments | a canvas, transcript or screenshot arrives as boilerplate text around a link, which Amos cannot act on — the underlying content still reaches it through knowledge ingest |
| Edits and typo fixes | an edit would re-run a whole turn |
| A bare `@Amos` with no message | a mention alone is not a prompt |
| Authors outside the workspace | a Slack Connect channel can carry outsiders |

## One thing at a time

- **One turn per thread at a time.** A second message while the first is
  running is answered with "still working on your previous message" rather
  than queued or interleaved.
- **An ack, then the answer.** No live progress in Slack — the answer arrives
  as a message when it is ready.
- **No slash commands and no file uploads yet.** A shared file gets a spoken
  refusal explaining that, not silence.
- **Recent context only.** Amos carries the last few completed turns of a
  thread, so a very long Slack conversation will feel slightly thinner than
  the transcript looks — Amos never reads back through Slack history itself.

## Who you are in Slack

A Slack message is matched to your **existing** Amos account by your
workspace-verified email address. Amos only ever matches — it never creates an
account on your behalf — and only for authors inside the configured workspace.
