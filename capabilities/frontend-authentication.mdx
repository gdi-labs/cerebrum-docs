## What it gives you

- **Google sign-in** for every user, plus **email + password** for people
  without a Google account (grant reviewers, demo users). Email sign-*up* is
  closed — an admin mints those accounts.
- **Invite-only access.** A new account can authenticate but lands *pending* —
  disabled until an admin approves it. You'll see a pending screen; ask your
  admin to expect you, then reload once approved.
- **Two roles.** Admins see and manage everything; members see only their own
  workflows.
- **Per-user monthly budgets.** Admins set a USD budget per member; once a
  member's month-to-date spend reaches the cap, new runs are refused until the
  month rolls over or the budget is raised. A running job is allowed to finish —
  only *new* submissions stop.
- **Account disable.** Admins can disable any account; its sessions end
  immediately.
- **An admin section** listing every user with role, status, budget, and
  month-to-date spend, plus inline controls for all of the above.

## Getting in

1. Choose **Sign in with Google** (or use the password form if an admin
   created an account for you — it's tucked behind the lock icon next to the
   Google button).
2. New account? You're pending until an admin approves you. The screen tells
   you so.
3. Approved admins are automatic: the addresses configured as admins are
   promoted on their first sign-in.

## What members and admins see

Members get the full product — submitting work, watching runs, approvals,
projects — scoped to their own workflows. Admins additionally get the admin
section: every user, with role/status/budget/spend and the controls to change
them. Every admin action is audit-logged.

## Budgets in practice

Your remaining budget and month-to-date spend are visible in the settings
panel. If a submission is refused with a budget error, you've hit the cap —
ask an admin to raise it in the admin section. Admins themselves are exempt.

## Sessions

One sign-in works across every Amos host (the app and its preview hosts share
the session), and signing out ends it everywhere.
